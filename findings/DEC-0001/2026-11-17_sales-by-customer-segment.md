# Sales and gross profit by customer segment, January 2013 to May 2016

(`claude-opus-5-5`), from the analysis
[`sales_by_customer_segment_month`](../../model/analyses/sales_by_customer_segment_month.md) in the warehouse
published on the author's machine, **as known on 2016-06-06** (the sandbox's simulated date). It serves
[DEC-0001](../../registers/decisions/DEC-0001_customer-segment-sales-attention.md): which customer segments get sales
attention. The objects that produced it were drafted and generated by Claude Fable 5.2; the figures were read by a
different model, which is a cross-check of the result, not a review of the code.

Wide World Importers is sample data generated by a script. Every pattern below may be the script's, not a market's;
this note shows that the chain from extract to answer works, and says what the answer would be if the data were real.

## Findings

1. **Growth has stopped.** Sales excluding tax were 45.7 million in 2013, 49.9 million in 2014 (+9.2%) and 54.0
   million in 2015 (+8.1%). January to May 2016 brought 22.63 million, against 22.68 million in the same months of 2015
   (−0.2%).
2. **Two buying groups carry most of the business.** Tailspin Toys and Wingtip Toys, each about 200 novelty shops, took
   31.6% and 31.5% of sales over the whole period. The five segments of independent customers (novelty shops,
   supermarkets, gift stores, computer stores, corporate) took 6.6% to 8.4% each.
3. **The buying groups grew slowest.** In 2015 they grew 4.5% and 4.7%, the independents between 8.8% (supermarkets)
   and 21.0% (gift stores). In January to May 2016 against the same months of 2015, Tailspin fell 1.4% and Wingtip
   1.2%; computer stores rose 10.2%, gift stores 3.3%, supermarkets 2.2%; independent novelty shops were level
   (−0.1%) and corporate customers fell 7.5%.
4. **Margin does not tell the segments apart.** Gross profit was 49.3% to 50.3% of sales in every segment over the
   whole period, and between 45.6% and 54.6% in any one segment in any one month. Part of this is how the source
   computes profit: against today's cost price, for every line since 2013 (point 7), so a change of cost over time
   cannot show.
5. **What this suggests for DEC-0001**, for the person who decides: attention would go first to the two buying groups,
   which are almost two thirds of sales and have stopped growing, and then to corporate customers, the only segment
   clearly falling in 2016. Computer stores are the one segment still growing at the 2014–2015 rate.

## How far the figures can be trusted

6. **The chain was checked row by row.** On the load of 2016-06-06 every generated test passed on the author's machine
   (816 tests, 13 objects added by the slice), among them the lineage test, which rebuilds every invoice line from the
   delivered rows by the source's own identifiers and compares it with the fact table, and the reconciliation of
   every measure with the delivery. No line was left unplaced: all 229,483 invoice lines have a customer and a date.
   As known on 2016-06-01 the fact table has 228,432 lines, the number the analysis record counted independently in
   that day's extract (`analysis/wwi/tables/Sales/InvoiceLines/07_population.md`, claim C1).
7. **Gross profit is at current cost**, the source's `LineProfit`, which equals quantity × (unit price − the stock
   item's cost price today) on every line (`analysis/wwi/tables/Sales/InvoiceLines/09_derived.md`, claim C3).
8. **Segments are as currently known** ([ADR-0039](../../docs/20_decisions/0039-first-use-case-and-where-marts-read-from.md)
   decision 4). No customer has ever changed category or buying group in this source, so nothing is restated here.
9. **Not in these figures:** June 2016, which has six days (1.0 million) and is marked incomplete; credit notes, of
   which the source has none; stock items and stock groups, left out of the first cut. Amounts are in one currency
   the source does not name (`analysis/wwi/tables/Sales/CustomerTransactions/08_measures.md`).
