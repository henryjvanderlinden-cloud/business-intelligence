# 030 — Glossary

Terms used with a specific meaning in this repository. Where a term already has a
standard meaning in data warehousing, that meaning is assumed and only the local
narrowing is recorded.

**Adjudication** — the act of a human confirming, rejecting or amending a hypothesis
raised by the system. Distinct from *instruction*: the system proposes, the human rules.

**Adjudication queue** — the ranked backlog of hypotheses awaiting human ruling,
ordered by expected information gain rather than by age.

**Artifact** — anything the system produces and stores: a card, a hypothesis, a
generated model, a test, a mart, an analysis. Every artifact has an identity, a
provenance and a lifecycle.

**Business key** — in the Data Vault sense: the identifier by which the business refers
to an entity, as opposed to a surrogate or technical key. Identifying these correctly
is the central semantic problem of the system.

**Card** — the unit of the context layer: a small, self-contained, version-controlled
document describing exactly one object (a source, a schema, a table, a column, a metric,
a decision), with structured front matter and prose. Sized to be cheap to load.

**Context layer** — the whole body of cards, hypotheses and indexes; the system's model
of the landscape. Called *metadata* in the brief, but the term is avoided here because
it suggests something incidental to the data rather than the primary product.

**Contract (source contract)** — the authorisation envelope for a data source: owner,
legal basis, classification, read budget, permitted operations.

**Decision register** — the record of decisions the organisation actually makes, which
anchors the relevance of automated analysis.

**Demand justification** — the reason an artifact is permitted to exist: a question it
answers, a named consumer, or an observed usage signal. Artifacts without one are
candidates for pruning.

**Falsifier** — the observation that would overturn an asserted fact. Recorded alongside
the assertion where one exists.

**Hypothesis** — a proposition generated by the system that is not yet established:
typically that two columns denote the same entity, that a column is a business key, that
a table is a satellite of a given hub. Hypotheses carry evidence and confidence and
either graduate to assertions or are retired.

**Information gain (of a question)** — the number and weight of downstream hypotheses
whose resolution depends on a given unanswered question. Used to rank the adjudication
queue.

**Landscape** — the totality of data sources available to the organisation, including
those not yet discovered.

**Provenance** — how a stated fact came to be known: `inferred` (from data),
`asserted` (by a named human), `documented` (from a named artifact), or `derived` (from
other facts). Every fact in the context layer carries one.

**Pruning** — the deliberate retirement of artifacts that have lost their demand
justification. Given equal standing with construction.

**Statability** — the property of a piece of knowledge that it can be expressed as a rule
a deterministic machine can apply correctly to cases it has not seen. Requires three
conditions together: *articulability* (it can be put into words at all), *generality*
(the statement covers cases not individually enumerated), and *stability* (it stays true
long enough to be worth encoding). Data semantics characteristically fail the second, not
the first — perfectly sayable, with no general rule that would produce them — which is
the distinction on which [005_manifesto.md](005_manifesto.md) §III turns. Not a standard
term; coined here, and related to but not identical with Polanyi's tacit knowledge.

**Structural / semantic / teleological** — the three layers of analysis distinguished in
[020_critique.md](020_critique.md), each with a different degree of permitted autonomy.
See [`../10_architecture/105_autonomy-tiers.md`](../10_architecture/105_autonomy-tiers.md).
