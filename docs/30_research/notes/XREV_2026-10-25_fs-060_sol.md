## Pass 1 — sol's answer, verbatim

1. Quoted sentence: “Cash collected but not yet swept to the issuer account is exposure to the servicer.”

   What is wrong: Location before a sweep does not by itself establish commingling exposure. A collection account may already be in the issuer's name, subject to trust or security, controlled by a trustee, or otherwise segregated from the servicer's estate. Conversely, cash may be exposed before it reaches that account, or remain exposed after a nominal sweep if the destination or control arrangement is defective. The sentence confuses a process stage with the legal and insolvency analysis.

   Correction: Determine exposure from the account holder, beneficial ownership, perfection and control, segregation, permitted commingling period, insolvency treatment, account-bank eligibility and any commingling reserve or guarantee. Report unswept cash as servicer exposure only where those facts make it part of, or vulnerable to, the servicer's estate.

   Confidence: High.

2. Quoted sentence: “Payments and transfers → accounts. Explains where cash sits along the structure: borrower → collection account → issuer account → waterfall.”

   What is wrong: A waterfall is not a place where cash sits or an account to which it moves. It is a priority-of-payments calculation that determines allocations and payment instructions. Also, a collection account can itself be an issuer account; the two labels do not necessarily denote successive accounts.

   Correction: Separate account custody from waterfall determination. Model cash events between actual accounts, and model each waterfall run, its calculation basis, priority items, allocations and resulting payment instructions separately. Attach legal and operational roles to accounts with effective dates rather than assuming “collection” and “issuer” are successive physical locations.

   Confidence: High.

3. Quoted sentence: “Make Account a hub covering every account in the structure: the servicer's collection account, the issuer transaction account, reserve accounts, GIC accounts.”

   What is wrong: This treats role names as though they identify physical accounts and treats reserve and GIC balances as necessarily distinct bank accounts. The same bank account can perform several roles, a role can migrate to another account after an account-bank downgrade, a reserve can be a designated ledger balance within an account, and a GIC is also a contractual investment claim rather than merely an account label. If a hub key is built from these role names, one physical account is duplicated or successive accounts are merged, breaking balances and exposure history.

   Correction: Key the account hub by a stable account identity within the account provider and source namespace. Model account holder, provider, currency and identifiers with historised satellites as appropriate; model effective-dated account roles and control/ownership relationships separately. Represent a reserve balance and a GIC/investment contract as their own business concepts when they are not coterminous with a bank account.

   Confidence: High.

4. Quoted sentence: “Model Transfers as a transactional link between a from-account and a to-account. Its attributes: amount, value date, booking date, transfer type: collection, sweep or waterfall payment.”

   What is wrong: A Data Vault link whose business key is only the from-account/to-account pair has relationship grain, not transfer-event grain. Every daily sweep between the same two accounts then hashes to the same link; simultaneous transfers and retransmissions cannot be distinguished, and amount/date satellites turn separate transactions into versions of one relationship. In addition, a direct-debit collection or waterfall payment is not necessarily an internal transfer between two accounts known to the vault.

   Correction: Give each instructed bank transaction or cash-movement event its own stable identity, sourced from bank transaction identifiers plus an explicit collision strategy, and link that event to accounts in typed debit/credit roles. Keep instruction, booking, value, settlement, rejection and reversal states distinct; store amount and currency at event grain. Model a payment instruction and its one or more bank postings separately when one instruction is split, netted or retried.

   Confidence: High.

5. Quoted sentence: “Model it as a link with an allocated-amount satellite.”

   What is wrong: A link keyed only by payment and posting collapses multiple allocation actions between the same pair. For example, an engine may allocate 100, unallocate it after a correction, and reallocate 80; a descriptive satellite can preserve load history but does not by itself express three business events or which one reverses which. A current-snapshot source can also make the initial allocation disappear before it was vaulted.

   Correction: Model an allocation event or allocation business key, linked to payment and posting, with business-effective time, currency, status and source transaction identity. Represent deallocation and reallocation as compensating events or explicitly effectivised states, so the net allocation and the audit trail are both derivable.

   Confidence: High.

6. Quoted sentence: “Model unallocated cash explicitly, as a suspense position, rather than leaving it as a gap.”

   What is wrong: A mutable “position” without the events that created and cleared it loses ageing and can double-count cash. If a receipt is first put into suspense and later allocated, retaining both the receipt's full allocation candidate and the suspense position makes total cash exceed the bank balance; overwriting the position destroys evidence of how long it was unresolved.

   Correction: Treat suspense as an account or ledger classification fed by immutable receipt, allocation, release, write-off and reversal events. Derive the open balance as of a business time, by account and currency, and age it from the unresolved event while preserving partial clearings and reopenings.

   Confidence: High.

7. Quoted sentence: “Reconcile sweeps at collection-period level: collections received = swept + in transit.”

   What is wrong: This is not a valid cash roll-forward. It omits opening unswept cash, closing retained balances, returns and refunds, bank charges, interest, adjustments, permitted deductions, reserve funding, failed sweeps and cut-off differences. “In transit” also conflates borrower payments not yet finally booked with an initiated sweep not yet credited. A day with a returned direct debit after the prior day's sweep fails the identity even when operations are correct.

   Correction: Reconcile each actual account by currency and bank cut-off using a roll-forward such as opening booked balance + booked credits − booked debits = closing booked balance, with sweeps, returns/refunds, fees, interest and adjustments separately classified. Reconcile initiated-but-unsettled transfers through their own status bridge between the debit and credit account postings. A collection-cohort reconciliation should be additional and must state whether “received” means instructed, booked, value-dated or settled.

   Confidence: High.

8. Quoted sentence: “Don't force loan-level lineage through an aggregate transfer. It only produces artificial allocations.”

   What is wrong: Rejecting invented pro-rata allocation is sound, but rejecting all loan-to-sweep lineage prevents a concrete control where the servicer supplies a sweep batch or remittance file that enumerates the collections included. Without that batch membership, an omitted loan receipt can be hidden by an equal over-remittance or adjustment and the aggregate reconciliation still passes.

   Correction: Preserve evidenced membership from collection events to a remittance or sweep batch when the source provides it, without inventing amount allocations that the source does not provide. Link the batch to the bank transfer and reconcile batch total, transfer amount and exceptions; stop at aggregate lineage only when no finer-grained evidence exists.

   Confidence: High.

9. Quoted sentence: “SEPA direct debits can be returned or refunded weeks after collection, so cash already swept may have to come back.”

   What is wrong: “Returned or refunded weeks after” conflates different SEPA outcomes and understates the tail. Under SEPA Core, an authorised debit can generally be refunded for eight weeks, while a claim for an unauthorised debit can be made for up to thirteen months. Scheme and interbank return timelines are different again. Under SEPA B2B, an authorised-debit refund right does not mirror Core. Moreover, a refund debit need not reverse the original sweep physically; it may hit the designated collection account and be funded by later collections, liquidity support, a reserve or the servicer under the transaction documents.

   Correction: Model reject, return, refund, reversal and revocation as distinct reasoned event types, with the applicable Core/B2B scheme and deadline. Record which account was actually debited and how the shortfall was funded; do not infer a reverse sweep merely because the original collection had been swept.

   Confidence: High.

10. Quoted sentence: “Model each reversal as a new event, linked to the original through the end-to-end ID or mandate reference.”

   What is wrong: Neither identifier is a reliable standalone correlation key. A mandate reference identifies a recurring mandate, not one debit. End-to-end IDs may be absent, populated as `NOTPROVIDED`, reused, truncated or transformed between creditor, clearing and account-statement records. Hard-linking on either field can attach a refund to the wrong instalment, especially where amount and due date repeat.

   Correction: Preserve all original and returned message references available from the scheme and bank, including original message/payment-information/instruction/end-to-end/transaction identifiers and the bank's account-servicer reference. Resolve the link with creditor, debtor, mandate, amount, currency, requested collection date and booking context; retain ambiguous or unmatched cases explicitly rather than forcing a match.

   Confidence: High.

11. Quoted sentence: “The account bank says what actually moved.”

   What is wrong: A camt.053 statement is the account servicer's end-of-day ledger assertion about booked entries. It does not prove economic finality, does not normally show all pending instructions, can contain aggregated entries requiring transaction detail, and can later be corrected or reversed. Calling it what “actually moved” causes unmatched pending sweeps to be treated as missing cash and bank corrections to be treated as new economics.

   Correction: Treat camt.053 as authoritative evidence of the bank's booked account entries for the statement period, subject to status, entry detail and later correction. Reconcile it to servicer records and, where timing matters, to available debit/credit notifications, intraday reports and payment-status messages; keep source disagreement rather than declaring either source physical truth.

   Confidence: High.

12. Quoted sentence: “Direct-debit reversal rate per collection period.”

   What is wrong: This metric is misleading unless it is cohort-defined and matured. Dividing reversals posted in a period by collections in that same period mixes reversals of older debits with new collections and structurally understates recent cohorts because Core refund claims can arrive for eight weeks and unauthorised claims much later. Combining technical returns, authorised refunds and unauthorised claims also obscures distinct operational causes; count and cash-value rates can tell opposite stories.

   Correction: Report separate return, authorised-refund and unauthorised-claim rates by original debit cohort, both by count and amount, with explicit seasoning/completeness and reason-code breakdowns. Show a posting-period cash-impact measure separately from the cohort performance rate.

   Confidence: High.

verdict - FAILS + an issuer-owned, secured collection account makes unswept cash not servicer commingling exposure, while the proposed account-pair transfer link collapses every recurring sweep into one relationship

## Pass 2 — sol's answer, verbatim

1. Quoted sentences: “Exposure depends on: account holder and beneficial ownership; security, trust or control arrangements; segregation; permitted commingling period; insolvency treatment...” and “Record these per account and role, with effective dates.”

   Finding 1 is only partially applied. The revision omits perfection or enforceability as a recorded fact. The existence of a security or trust arrangement does not establish that the interest is perfected against the servicer's insolvency estate. Failure case: the issuer has a security assignment over a servicer-held collection account, but a required notice, registration or control step was not completed before the servicer's insolvency. The proposed attributes can make that account look identical to one under a perfected assignment even though the cash has different commingling exposure. Record the perfection/enforceability state, its jurisdiction and effective time as part of the applicable legal arrangement, rather than treating the existence of “security” as sufficient.

2. Quoted sentences: “Key the Account hub by real account identity: account identifier within the account bank and source namespace” and “Holder, provider and currency go in historised satellites.”

   The key and the provider satellite are inconsistent. If the account bank scopes the business key, a change of provider cannot be a historised descriptive change on the same hub identity; it creates a different scoped identity. Failure case: local account identifier `12345` exists at Bank A and Bank B. The scoped keys correctly distinguish them, but changing the provider satellite on Bank A's hub to Bank B either falsely preserves one account across two providers or makes the hub key cease to describe the record. Conversely, if an account is renumbered while remaining legally the same account, keying only on the current identifier fractures its history because the rewrite dropped the earlier qualification that identifiers may themselves need historisation. The model needs an explicit identity rule for provider migration and identifier change, with same-as/supersession treatment where the source does not supply a durable account identity.

3. Quoted sentences: “Each cash movement is its own event with a stable identity: the bank transaction identifier, plus an explicit rule for collisions” and “A camt.053 ... can contain batched entries that need their transaction detail.”

   Finding 4 is applied too narrowly: a bank transaction identifier is not guaranteed to exist at the asserted cash-movement grain, and a collision rule only resolves duplicate values, not absence or grain mismatch. Failure case: a camt.053 contains one aggregate entry for a direct-debit batch and omits `NtryDtls/TxDtls`; it supplies an entry reference for the aggregate but no identifier for each underlying collection. Another failure case is a transfer whose debit and credit postings carry different account-servicer references and no common bank identifier. The required event key therefore cannot be constructed without inventing identity or falsely treating a batch as one movement. Source statement entries and transaction details should have identities at their own reported grains; an instruction or cross-account movement should be a separate event linked by explicit, possibly unresolved matching evidence.

4. Quoted sentences: “These are distinct event types, each with a reason code, scheme (Core or B2B) and deadline” and “Under SEPA Core, an authorised debit can be refunded for eight weeks, and an unauthorised debit can be claimed for up to thirteen months.”

   Finding 9 is only partially applied. The rewrite states that every type has an applicable deadline but records only the two Core refund-notification windows. It gives no applicable Core/B2B timing rule for rejects, returns, reversals or revocations, and does not say that the deadline must be stored or derived from the rulebook version, settlement date and interbank-business-day calendar. Failure case: a post-settlement technical return is tested against the eight-week authorised-refund window and reported as timely even though the scheme's much shorter return deadline has expired. The eight-week and thirteen-month periods also need their anchor stated: they run from the debit date, and the unauthorised-payment notification is subject to the payer notifying its PSP without undue delay as well as no later than thirteen months. For a non-consumer B2B payer, the framework contract may disapply the statutory notification rule, so “up to thirteen months” must not be inherited from Core merely because the event is labelled unauthorised.

5. Quoted heading: “Linking a return to the original debit.”

   Finding 10 is only partially applied. After distinguishing return, refund and reversal as different event types, the correlation model is stated only for a return. Failure case: an authorised Core refund arrives with original-message and original-transaction references, but the implementation limits the resolution relationship to events typed `return`; the refund remains unlinked, so the original-debit cohort metric and the funding trace are wrong. Define the original-debit relationship for every dependent exception event that carries or requires such a reference, including refunds and reversals, while retaining ambiguous and unmatched resolutions.

verdict - FAILS + an unperfected security assignment is indistinguishable from a perfected one, while a batched camt.053 entry can provide no transaction identifier at the mandated cash-movement grain

## Disposition

   - Commingling exposure is treated as a legal determination, not a location.
   - The waterfall is a calculation, not a place.
   - Accounts are keyed by real identity, with effective-dated roles.
   - Reserves and GICs are their own concepts.
   - Cash movements have event-level identity.
   - Allocation and suspense are event-based.
   - Reconciliation is a per-account booked-balance roll-forward.
   - Evidenced sweep-batch membership is kept.
