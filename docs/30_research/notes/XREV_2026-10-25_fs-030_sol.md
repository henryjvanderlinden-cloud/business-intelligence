## Pass 1 — sol's answer, verbatim

1. **Quoted sentence:** “The LDM is shaped for integration, which is what a raw vault needs.”

   **What is wrong:** A Raw Vault is not a canonical integration model in the BIRD sense. It preserves source assertions at their original grain and meaning, adding business-key integration and audit metadata with minimal reinterpretation. Forcing a source into BIRD’s reporting-oriented LDM before the Raw Vault makes the conformance transformation part of ingestion. A source record that has no lossless BIRD representation can then be rejected, collapsed, or assigned an invented meaning before its original assertion has been retained.

   **Correction:** Land each source losslessly and model its business keys, relationships, descriptive context, source record identifier, load time, and record source in the Raw Vault. Implement BIRD conformance as an explicitly lineage-bearing Business Vault or downstream canonical layer unless a source already emits data at exactly the BIRD grain and semantics.

   **Confidence:** High.

2. **Quoted sentence:** “Entity with a stable identifier | Hub”

   **What is wrong:** A stable identifier is insufficient to make an LDM entity a Hub. A Hub represents a durable business concept identified by a business key; associative, dependent, classification, snapshot, and context entities can have stable technical or composite identifiers without being independent business concepts. Turning every such entity into a Hub creates invented business objects and severs their identifying relationships. For example, an instrument-accounting observation identified by instrument, reference date, accounting standard, and consolidation scope would become a purported independent object even though none of those observations exists independently of the instrument and context.

   **Correction:** Classify each LDM entity by semantic identity and grain. Create Hubs only for independently identifiable business concepts; represent relationships as Links, descriptive history as Satellites, and repeating/dependent grains with an appropriate Link, dependent-child key, or multi-active/snapshot pattern.

   **Confidence:** High.

3. **Quoted sentence:** “Associative entity / role | Link”

   **What is wrong:** Neither every associative entity nor a role automatically maps one-for-one to a Data Vault Link. A Link records an association among Hub business keys at a declared immutable grain. A role may be a qualifier of that association, a relationship type, or descriptive/effective context; an associative entity may itself carry a source business key or represent an event. If “debtor” is omitted from the Link identity, the same party–instrument pair acting concurrently as debtor and protection provider collapses to one Link; if every role change is instead encoded in the Link hash indiscriminately, a mutable classification manufactures a new relationship and breaks continuity.

   **Correction:** Determine the source relationship grain first. Put role in Link identity only when it distinguishes concurrent business relationships; otherwise historise it in a Satellite. Model an associative entity with its own durable business key as a Hub plus Links, or as a transaction/event Link, as its semantics require.

   **Confidence:** High.

4. **Quoted sentence:** “The raw/business vault split lines up with BIRD's own LDM/ELDM split.”

   **What is wrong:** These splits answer different questions. LDM versus ELDM distinguishes base from enriched/derived BIRD concepts; Raw Vault versus Business Vault distinguishes auditable source capture from governed derivation and integration. An LDM value can already be the result of a bank’s semantic transformation and therefore belong outside the Raw Vault transformation boundary, while an ELDM-like value supplied verbatim by a source must still be retained as a raw source assertion. Equating the pairs loses both source provenance and the distinction between “received” and “calculated here.”

   **Correction:** Assign content to Raw or Business Vault from provenance and transformation lineage, not from its BIRD layer. Retain received values unchanged in Raw Vault and materialise BIRD conformance or derivations in Business Vault, recording the rule, inputs, and run.

   **Confidence:** High.

5. **Quoted sentence:** “BIRD has one time axis: the reference date (`DT_RFRNC`), a reporting-period snapshot.”

   **What is wrong:** This is too strong as a statement about the BIRD LDM. Reference date is the principal reporting/snapshot coordinate, but BIRD also carries domain dates and periods such as inception, maturity, default/status, valuation, and other applicability dates. Those dates are not interchangeable with `DT_RFRNC`; treating them as ordinary non-temporal descriptions loses the distinction between the date on which a condition applied and the reporting date on which it was observed.

   **Correction:** State that BIRD is primarily organised by reporting reference date and does not provide a general system-time history. Preserve each domain-effective date or period according to its defined semantics as well as the reference-date snapshot coordinate.

   **Confidence:** Medium.

6. **Quoted sentence:** “Data Vault supplies transaction time natively, through load timestamps.”

   **What is wrong:** A Data Vault load timestamp is warehouse record/arrival time. It is not automatically source-system transaction time, report-submission time, or the interval during which a regulator or engine regarded an assertion as current. A correction effective on 31 March but received on 15 May has a 15 May warehouse load time; a late replay loaded on 20 May may have been known to the source on 1 April. Calling those timestamps transaction time makes an “as reported on 10 April” query return the wrong state.

   **Correction:** Name load time as warehouse system time and use it as the second bitemporal axis only if that is the intended meaning and immutable versions are retained. Capture source assertion/commit time and regulatory submission or acceptance identifiers and timestamps separately when “as known” or “as reported” refers to those events.

   **Confidence:** High.

7. **Quoted sentence:** “Valid time has to be added: valid-from/valid-to in satellites; effectivity satellites on links.”

   **What is wrong:** This presents two storage recipes as a complete valid-time model. A standard Effectivity Satellite is used to track the active period of a relationship—normally under a declared driving-key rule—not to make every Link bitemporal. It also cannot manufacture valid intervals from periodic snapshots. If a monthly file omits an instrument in April because the extract is incomplete, closing the March relationship at the next observed boundary falsely records a business termination; if two counterparties are valid concurrently, a single-driving-key effectivity rule may incorrectly end one when the other arrives.

   **Correction:** Define valid-time semantics and source evidence per relationship and attribute. Use an Effectivity Satellite only where the source supplies or supports relationship effectivity and the driving-key/cardinality rule is explicit; otherwise preserve dated observations, and distinguish “not observed” from “ceased.” Use historised or multi-active Satellites where concurrent valid versions are legitimate.

   **Confidence:** High.

8. **Quoted sentence:** “The reference date becomes an attribute, not the temporal backbone.”

   **What is wrong:** For BIRD snapshot facts, reference date is part of the observation grain, not merely a descriptive attribute. A Satellite normally permits one row per parent key and load timestamp (subject to the chosen Data Vault pattern). Loading several historical BIRD reference dates for the same instrument in one batch therefore collides at the Satellite key or forces arbitrary deduplication if `DT_RFRNC` is only an attribute. Even when separate load times avoid a physical collision, an as-of-load query can confuse the latest-arriving old snapshot with the latest reporting period.

   **Correction:** Preserve `DT_RFRNC` as an explicit snapshot/effective coordinate. Where more than one reference-date observation can arrive for a parent in one load, use a declared periodic-snapshot, multi-active, or dependent-child grain and keep warehouse load time as a separate axis.

   **Confidence:** High.

9. **Quoted sentence:** “**Party–instrument role** (‘has as role in instrument’). Data Vault: link with an effectivity satellite.”

   **What is wrong:** This mapping assumes without establishing both the Link grain and a single relationship-effectivity rule. Party–instrument roles can be concurrent and role-qualified, and the party–instrument association may have additional identifying context. A naïve two-Hub Link collapses two simultaneous roles for the same party and instrument; a naïve Effectivity Satellite driven by instrument can also close an existing debtor relationship when a second debtor is added rather than when the first debtor ceases.

   **Correction:** Include every relationship-grain discriminator needed to distinguish concurrent associations, or model role-qualified relationships separately. Add effectivity only from explicit start/end evidence and declare the driving key and cardinality; do not infer replacement from the arrival of another party–instrument row unless the source guarantees exclusivity.

   **Confidence:** High.

10. **Quoted sentence:** “**Instrument-keyed data sets.** Financial data, accounting data, risk and default data. Data Vault: satellites on the Instrument hub, not hubs.”

   **What is wrong:** “Instrument-keyed” does not mean “one row per instrument.” Such observations commonly vary by reference date and by contexts such as reporting agent, counterparty/role, accounting standard, consolidation scope, currency, scenario, model, or horizon. Putting all of them directly in ordinary Instrument Satellites loses those discriminators or produces multiple active rows at the same parent/load key. A PD for a debtor–instrument relationship can then overwrite an instrument-level PD, or consolidated and solo accounting values can collapse into one value.

   **Correction:** Determine the complete business grain of each data set. Attach genuinely instrument-level single-valued descriptions to Instrument Satellites; represent relationship-dependent measures on the relevant Link Satellite and repeating contextual observations with explicit context Hubs/Links or a justified multi-active/dependent-child pattern.

   **Confidence:** High.

11. **Quoted sentence:** “Put them in their own satellites on the Instrument hub, with the engine as record source.”

   **What is wrong:** Finance and risk outputs do not share a universal instrument grain. Stage or impairment may be instrument-and-accounting-scope observations, while PD/LGD can be counterparty, facility, rating, collateral, scenario, horizon, model-run, or allocation outputs. `record source = engine` identifies provenance but not calculation grain. Two scenarios from the same engine and run can consequently collide, while a correction from a later run can appear to be ordinary descriptive history rather than a distinct calculation result.

   **Correction:** Model each output at its full calculation grain, including the relevant party/instrument/protection relationship, reference date, scenario or horizon, run, and model version. Use Satellites only after the parent grain is correct; use a calculation-run Hub and Links when runs are durable, shared lineage objects rather than mere row attributes.

   **Confidence:** High.

12. **Quoted sentence:** “Why bother: regulatory resubmissions need both ‘as reported then’ and ‘as corrected now’.”

   **What is wrong:** The proposed two dates do not by themselves answer either phrase. “As reported then” is versioned by report/submission identity, reporting perimeter, and submission/acceptance time; “correct for 31 March” needs an effective/reference date plus the system or assertion time at which the correction became known. Two submissions can be loaded in the same warehouse batch, and one submission can contain corrections for several reference dates. Valid-from/to plus a warehouse load timestamp cannot distinguish those cases.

   **Correction:** Retain submission/report version as a business object or explicit grain, link every reported fact to it, and record submitted/accepted timestamps separately from warehouse load time. Then combine that lineage with the fact’s BIRD reference date or other valid-time interval.

   **Confidence:** High.

verdict - FAILS + a late regulatory resubmission loaded alongside multiple historical reference dates cannot be reconstructed “as reported then” from Instrument Satellites keyed only by Hub and load timestamp

## Pass 2 — sol's answer, verbatim

1. **Quoted sentence:** “Use [BIRD's LDM] to name and identify hubs (party, instrument, protection) and the relationships between them, so different sources integrate on the same business keys.”

   **Failure:** The LDM can supply a shared concept and grain, but it does not establish that source identifiers inhabit the same business-key domain. Integration requires the key's issuer or collision domain to be resolved independently. If reporting agents A and B both supply `INSTRMNT_ID = 123` and that identifier is unique only within reporting agent, treating the BIRD-named attribute as a shared business key merges two instruments. Conversely, two identifiers for the same instrument remain separate without an asserted crosswalk. The sentence partially reintroduces the pre-Raw-Vault conformance error that finding 1 removed: common BIRD terminology is being allowed to imply source-key integration.

2. **Quoted sentence:** “An associative entity with its own durable business key becomes a hub plus links.”

   **Failure:** A durable identifier does not make a relationship an independently identifiable business concept, so this repeats finding 2's rejected `stable identifier → Hub` test for associative entities. Suppose a source gives each party–instrument assignment an immutable `ASSIGNMENT_ID`, but the assignment has no existence apart from that relationship. The categorical rule creates an Assignment Hub even though the identifier is a dependent-child or transaction discriminator of the Link. The following event exception does not repair the non-event case. The choice still has to follow semantic identity: Hub plus Links only when the associative object is independently meaningful; otherwise use the identifier at the appropriate Link/dependent-child grain.

3. **Quoted sentence:** “Where several versions are legitimately valid at once (two debtors), use multi-active or historised satellites, so the second debtor's arrival doesn't close the first.”

   **Failure:** Two debtors are concurrent relationships, not concurrent versions of one relationship's satellite. For instrument I with debtors A and B, the vault needs distinct role-qualified Link instances `(A, I, debtor)` and `(B, I, debtor)`. A multi-active Satellite on one party–instrument Link cannot add the other party because a Satellite cannot change its parent's Hub references; a historised Satellite likewise only versions attributes of its existing parent. Implementing the stated remedy either loses B or stores a foreign business key as descriptive data. This is a partial misapplication of findings 7 and 9.

4. **Quoted sentence:** “Add effectivity only from explicit start/end evidence, with a declared driving key; never infer that one party replaced another merely because a second one appeared.”

   **Failure:** “Explicit start/end evidence” is narrower than the source-supplied-or-supported test required by findings 7 and 9. A complete daily snapshot may contractually guarantee exactly one primary servicer per instrument while supplying no end-date field. When snapshot N contains servicer A and snapshot N+1 contains only servicer B, the completeness and exclusivity guarantees support ending A at the new snapshot boundary. Requiring an explicit end event leaves both relationships active. The prohibition needs the omitted qualification: do not infer replacement from mere arrival unless completeness and the declared cardinality make absence/replacement evidence.

5. **Quoted sentences:** “Observations that repeat by context (accounting standard, consolidation scope, currency, scenario) need that context in their key.” “Model the submission or report version as a business object and link every reported fact to it.”

   **Failure:** These sentences identify necessary discriminators but do not place them in a valid Data Vault parent grain. An ordinary Instrument Satellite has no independent business key, and a Link cannot target an individual Satellite row. If solo and consolidated carrying amounts for the same instrument and reference date are ordinary Satellite rows, adding `consolidation_scope` informally to “their key” either mutates the Instrument Hub key and splits one instrument into two Hubs, or leaves the rows colliding. Linking Submission S only to the Instrument does not identify which of several amount rows S reported; two submissions for the same instrument can both appear linked to both values. Findings 10 and 12 are therefore only partially applied. The contextual observation must be given an explicit Link, dependent-child, multi-active, or transactional grain, and submission identity must participate in or unambiguously identify that observation grain before the fact can be associated with a submission.

verdict - FAILS + two concurrent debtors cannot be represented as multi-active versions of a Satellite on one party–instrument Link

## Disposition

1. Pass 1, all twelve findings accepted, and the note rewritten around them. The main changes:
   - The raw vault stays source-aligned; BIRD guides hub and link naming and business-vault conformance.
   - Hubs only for independently identifiable concepts.
   - Roles go into link identity only where they distinguish concurrent relationships.
   - Raw versus business vault is decided by provenance, not BIRD layer.
   - The reference date is part of the observation grain.
   - Load time is warehouse system time.
   - Valid time is defined per relationship from source evidence.
   - Submission versions are modelled for "as reported".
   - Engine outputs are kept at their full calculation grain.

   - The LDM names concepts but doesn't settle key domains.
   - Associative entities become hubs only when independently meaningful.
   - Concurrent debtors are separate link instances.
   - Ends of relationships may be inferred from completeness plus declared cardinality.
   - Contextual observations and submissions need an explicit grain.
