## Pass 1 — sol's answer, verbatim

1. Quoted sentence: “BIRD is a methodology and data model for regulatory reporting.”

   What is wrong: This collapses several different things into one model and makes BIRD sound like a reporting regime. BIRD is not itself a regulatory requirement or a single database model. It is a voluntary, collaboratively maintained bank-side dictionary and methodology containing semantic definitions, logical modelling artefacts, input requirements, mappings and transformation rules for producing regulatory outputs.

   Correction: Describe BIRD as a voluntary integrated reporting dictionary and transformation methodology, with several model artefacts, intended to help banks derive regulatory reports from consistently defined input data.

   Confidence: High.

2. Quoted sentence: “It is developed by the ECB, euro-area national central banks and banking software vendors.”

   What is wrong: The industry side is not limited to software vendors. Banks and banking-industry representatives are central participants; reducing them to vendors misstates BIRD's governance and the provenance of its requirements.

   Correction: Say that BIRD is maintained through cooperation between the ESCB/ECB and the banking industry, including banks and industry associations as well as service or software providers.

   Confidence: High.

3. Quoted sentence: “BIRD and IReF (the ECB's Integrated Reporting Framework) share the same data dictionary and modelling approach.”

   What is wrong: BIRD and IReF are complementary and are being aligned, but they are not one dictionary or one model. IReF defines an integrated ESCB statistical reporting framework and collection requirements; BIRD describes bank-side input data and transformations that can support IReF and other reporting frameworks. Treating their semantics and models as identical would erase mappings, version differences and different governance boundaries.

   Correction: Say that IReF specifies harmonised statistical reporting requirements while BIRD can provide a complementary bank-side semantic and transformation layer; alignment and reuse do not imply a shared, identical dictionary or modelling approach.

   Confidence: High.

4. Quoted sentence: “The ECB treats them as the two pillars of its long-term reporting strategy.”

   What is wrong: “Two pillars” suggests equal status and a single governed architecture. IReF is an ESCB reporting framework, whereas BIRD is a voluntary industry collaboration intended to help reporting agents implement requirements. They are commonly presented as complementary initiatives, not interchangeable architectural layers.

   Correction: Call them complementary initiatives on the reporting-requirement and reporting-agent sides, and preserve their separate scope, adoption and governance.

   Confidence: Medium.

5. Quoted sentence: “Everything is published free:”

   What is wrong: The public BIRD artefacts are available without a licence fee, but “everything” is too broad for architectural reliance. It does not mean that every regulation, national extension, implementation artefact, executable transformation or vendor component is supplied, current and governed as one freely deployable package.

   Correction: Say that the ECB publishes BIRD documentation and model metadata publicly; verify the coverage, version, licence and executable implementation of each artefact separately.

   Confidence: High.

6. Quoted sentence: “Logical Data Model (LDM). Highly normalised and designed for integrity and consistency. This is the layer to build on.”

   What is wrong: The LDM is a technology-independent semantic/logical model, not a prescribed physical warehouse layer. Its normalisation is useful for defining concepts and relationships, but it does not settle persistence, history, performance, source alignment, keys or warehouse grain. “Build on” invites an architect to deploy the publication model as an operational schema without those decisions.

   Correction: Use the LDM as a semantic reference and mapping target. Derive a physical warehouse design only after separately deciding grain, identifiers, historisation, source-of-record rules, update semantics and performance requirements.

   Confidence: High.

7. Quoted sentence: “Enriched LDM (ELDM). The LDM plus derived attributes needed for reporting.”

   What is wrong: The ELDM is not simply an LDM with calculated reporting measures appended. Its enrichment exposes properties obtainable by navigating LDM relationships so that transformation rules can be stated against a flatter, usable context. An enriched property can be propagated from a related entity rather than calculated, while actual derivations remain transformation rules.

   Correction: Describe the ELDM as a mechanically enriched view of LDM entity types, adding relationship-derived paths/properties for rule authoring; distinguish those navigated properties from calculated reporting variables.

   Confidence: High.

8. Quoted sentence: “Example: an SME indicator derived from employee count and turnover.”

   What is wrong: This presents a regulatory classification calculation as if it exemplified ELDM enrichment. A rule that classifies an enterprise from thresholds and other conditions is a derivation; it is not the defining LDM-to-ELDM enrichment operation.

   Correction: Use a property brought into an entity's context through an LDM relationship as the ELDM example, and place an SME-classification calculation in the transformation-rule layer.

   Confidence: High.

9. Quoted sentence: “Input Layer (IL). BIRD's implementation model, derived from the LDM. Its tables are called ‘input cubes’.”

   What is wrong: The IL is a logical interface expected from a reporting bank, not a prescribed database implementation. A BIRD cube is a multidimensional model object defined by dimensions and variables; it need not be implemented one-for-one as a relational table. The IL is generated through the BIRD modelling chain, including ELDM choices, rather than being a physicalisation of the complete LDM.

   Correction: Describe the IL as BIRD's logical bank-input interface, organised into input cubes. Do not infer physical tables or a warehouse schema from cube boundaries.

   Confidence: High.

10. Quoted sentence: “Input cubes carry a reference date (`DT_RFRNC`). Data is a snapshot per reporting period.”

    What is wrong: The variable identifier is reversed; BIRD/ECB naming uses `RFRNC_DT`, not `DT_RFRNC`. More importantly, a reference date is a reporting-context dimension, not proof that every record is a complete periodic snapshot with identical update semantics.

    Correction: Use `RFRNC_DT` for the reference-date variable and model each cube's stated grain and temporal semantics rather than imposing one universal snapshot grain.

    Confidence: High.

11. Quoted sentence: “BIRD is stock-based: positions at a date, not events.”

    What is wrong: Regulatory outputs are heavily reference-date based, but BIRD also carries dates, changes, flows and event-like facts needed by particular frameworks. The sentence wrongly licenses deletion of transaction, validity and event history from a warehouse. Absence of a generic transaction-time axis in an output or input cube is not evidence that upstream events are out of scope.

    Correction: Say that BIRD is primarily organised around regulatory reference dates and periods. Preserve source-event and historisation data independently where needed for reconstruction, correction, lineage or flow reporting.

    Confidence: High.

12. Quoted sentence: “BIRD is fed by both back/mid-office systems and finance/risk calculation engines.”

    What is wrong: BIRD specifies information expected at its input boundary, not a mandatory source-system topology. The same variable may come from a ledger, accounting subledger, risk engine, master-data service or governed warehouse, and BIRD does not establish which is authoritative.

    Correction: Treat the named systems as plausible source categories only. Define system of record, ownership, reconciliation and lineage for each input variable locally.

    Confidence: High.

13. Quoted sentence: “Standard input-layer measures of this kind include: accumulated impairment (`ACCMLTD_IMPRMNT`); fair value (`FV`); accumulated changes in fair value (`ACCMLTD_CHNG_FV`).”

    What is wrong: `ACCMLTD_IMPRMNT` is a recognisable BIRD/AnaCredit-style input variable, but `FV` and `ACCMLTD_CHNG_FV` are not reliable exact BIRD variable identifiers as written. The AnaCredit-related measure is specifically accumulated changes in fair value due to credit risk, commonly coded `ACCMLTD_CHNGS_FV_D_CRDT_RSK`, not generic accumulated fair-value change. Exact variable availability also depends on the BIRD release and cube.

    Correction: Retain `ACCMLTD_IMPRMNT` only after checking the applicable release; replace the generic fair-value-change claim with the release-specific variable, including its “due to credit risk” scope, and obtain the exact fair-value variable name from that same release rather than using `FV` as if it were canonical.

    Confidence: Medium.

14. Quoted sentence: “BIRD derives reporting-specific constructs itself. The technical guidelines contain derivation rules for: carrying amount; exposure class; risk weight.”

    What is wrong: This treats three unlike quantities as universally BIRD-derived. Carrying amount is itself an input attribute in important BIRD/AnaCredit contexts even if particular outputs or variants can be calculated from components. Exposure class can be rule-derived only where the applicable framework supplies sufficient deterministic classification rules and inputs. Risk weight cannot generally be derived by a common BIRD rule: under IRB and other bank-specific treatments it depends on approved models, parameters, supervisory permissions and calculations supplied by the institution; even standardised treatment is framework- and version-specific.

    Correction: Classify variables per release, cube, framework and approach. Store an explicitly sourced carrying amount where the IL requires it; derive exposure class only under the relevant published rules; and treat bank/model-dependent risk-weight results or parameters as governed inputs unless a specific BIRD transformation demonstrably computes the applicable value.

    Confidence: High.

15. Quoted sentence: “LTV is usually on the derived side.”

    What is wrong: There is no single context-free LTV in BIRD. Current, origination, indexed, regulatory and reporting-template LTVs can differ in numerator, collateral denominator, valuation date, prior liens, allocation and aggregation. Some can be outputs of a specified transformation; others are reported or locally calculated inputs. “Usually” is not a safe warehouse rule.

    Correction: Model each named LTV definition separately, retain its numerator, denominator, valuation basis and as-of date, and classify it as input or derived only from the applicable BIRD or national-model rule.

    Confidence: High.

16. Quoted sentence: “It is computed from instrument amounts and protection data (protection value, valuation approach, allocated protection value per instrument–protection pair).”

    What is wrong: Those fields are not sufficient to define LTV. The calculation also depends on which exposure amount and collateral value definition apply, the valuation/reference date, senior claims or lien rank, eligible collateral scope, allocation rules, currency treatment and whether the metric is current or at origination. Allocated protection value is itself often the result of a non-trivial allocation rather than a neutral raw denominator.

    Correction: Implement only a named LTV formula from the applicable framework and preserve all definition and allocation inputs; do not derive a generic LTV from the listed fields.

    Confidence: High.

17. Quoted sentence: “National extensions sometimes take origination LTV as a raw attribute. Check the DNB residential real-estate model specifically.”

    What is wrong: This combines an unsupported generalisation with an unresolved design instruction. A national reference model may define an origination-LTV field, components, or both, but that does not establish that BIRD has a national “extension” or that the field is raw rather than calculated upstream.

    Correction: Treat the DNB RRE definition as a separately governed Dutch data-model item. Verify its field definition and lineage, and map it explicitly to any BIRD concept; do not inherit input/derived status from the label alone.

    Confidence: Medium.

18. Quoted sentence: “DNB publishes logical data models in BIRD style for: Residential real estate (RRE); Commercial real estate (CRE).”

    What is wrong: “In BIRD style” obscures status and compatibility. DNB's RRE and CRE artefacts are Dutch data reference/logical models that reuse or align with elements of BIRD modelling; they are not BIRD releases and need not have identical scope, versions or semantics.

    Correction: Describe them as separately governed DNB RRE and CRE data reference models with BIRD alignment/reuse, and require explicit mappings and version control between them and BIRD.

    Confidence: High.

19. Quoted sentence: “They are useful Dutch reference implementations.”

    What is wrong: A logical/reference data model is not an implementation. Calling it one encourages an architect to infer deployable schemas, ingestion contracts and operational behaviour that the publications do not provide.

    Correction: Call them Dutch reference models for the relevant data requests or domains, not reference implementations.

    Confidence: High.

20. Quoted sentence: “Entity names seen in them: party; protection received; instrument-protection received data; debtor-instrument data; financial data; accounting data.”

    What is wrong: This list mixes core entities, associations and context-specific data groupings/cubes. It gives no keys, cardinalities, grains or versioned definitions, so using every label as a warehouse entity would create false entity boundaries and likely duplicate measures.

    Correction: Classify each published object by model type—entity, relationship/association, cube or data group—and copy its formal grain, identifiers and cardinalities before mapping it to warehouse structures.

    Confidence: High.

## Unverified items

Quoted claim: “IFRS 9 stage, default status, PD and LGD are likewise engine outputs taken as inputs. They come from the AnaCredit-derived parts of the model **(unverified at attribute level)**.”

Best answer: The claim is only partly correct. Default status and counterparty probability of default are AnaCredit data attributes represented as supplied inputs in the AnaCredit-related BIRD content. `ACCMLTD_IMPRMNT` and impairment-related accounting status are also supplied accounting facts, but an IFRS 9 stage should not be equated without checking the release with AnaCredit's impairment-status categories. LGD is not an AnaCredit attribute and therefore cannot be said to come from an AnaCredit-derived part merely because it is used elsewhere in credit-risk reporting. More generally, BIRD does not require all four to originate in a “risk engine”: source and authority are institution-specific, while IFRS 9 stage is ordinarily an accounting/impairment classification. The warehouse should model their separate provenance, model/version date and as-of date.

Confidence: High for default status and PD being AnaCredit inputs and LGD not being an AnaCredit attribute; Medium for the exact release-level representation of IFRS 9 stage.

verdict - FAILS + the file treats BIRD's semantic LDM/ELDM/IL artefacts as warehouse layers and falsely generalises carrying amount, risk weight and LTV as BIRD-derived measures

## Pass 2 — sol's answer, verbatim

1. Quoted sentence: “**Engine outputs taken as inputs.** The Technical Guidelines 4.0 list these input-layer measures: accumulated impairment (`ACCMLTD_IMPRMNT`); fair value (`FV`); accumulated changes in fair value (`ACCMLTD_CHNG_FV`).”

   Finding #12 is misapplied. A measure's presence in the input layer establishes that BIRD expects the value at its input boundary; it does not establish that the value is an “engine output”. This heading reinstates the source-topology inference that the preceding three paragraphs correctly disclaim. Accumulated impairment or fair value may, for example, be supplied by an accounting sub-ledger, valuation service or governed warehouse. Call these input-layer accounting or valuation measures and leave their producing system to institution-specific lineage.

2. Quoted sentence: “Risk weights under internal-ratings-based (IRB) approaches come from the bank's approved models and are governed inputs, not BIRD calculations.”

   This new qualification is factually too broad. Under IRB, approved internal models estimate some risk parameters; they do not in general emit the regulatory risk weight. The regulatory risk-weight functions calculate risk-weighted exposure from prescribed formulae and parameters. The distinction is visible even within IRB: under foundation IRB the institution estimates PD while supervisory values supply other parameters, whereas advanced IRB permits additional internal estimates. Consequently, an applicable BIRD transformation may calculate a risk weight from governed inputs, or a particular cube may require a calculated result as an input; release, framework, exposure class and approach decide which. The sentence should say that IRB model parameters and any externally calculated results require governed provenance, and that BIRD may be called the calculator only where the applicable release contains the complete transformation.

3. Quoted sentence: “**Risk parameters.** Default status and the counterparty's probability of default (PD) are AnaCredit attributes, supplied as inputs. [...] IFRS 9 stage is an accounting impairment classification. [...] Record each parameter's source, model version and as-of date.”

   The rewrite now groups unlike facts under “parameters” and requires a model version for all of them. PD may be model-estimated, but default status and IFRS 9 stage are classifications whose governing rule, assessment evidence and effective/as-of date matter; they need not have a model version. LGD, discussed in the same group, can be model-estimated but is expressly not claimed here as an AnaCredit attribute. Record provenance and temporal scope for every item, and model or methodology version only where the value was actually produced under one.

verdict - FAILS + under IRB an approved model may estimate PD while the regulatory risk-weight function, not that model, calculates the risk weight
