## sol's answer, verbatim


## Finding 1

**Quoted sentence.** “Rule 1: the key is the source's own identifier, qualified by the key space `wwi`, unless the record shows a governed namespace.”

**What is wrong.** This turns a source primary key into a Data Vault business key by default. A primary-key declaration proves row identity in one database instance, not durable business identity. It also contradicts 144 §3 item 2 at the collision-code boundary: that rule says the code names the issuer, not the system, whereas 147 says `wwi` names “the WWI application database.” Neither the table-specific identity generators nor their lifetime across restores, migrations, or replacement applications are established.

**Concrete failure case.** WWI migrates customers to a replacement database that starts its identity sequence again. Old customer 42 and new, unrelated customer 42 hash to the same hub key under (`wwi`, 42); alternatively, if the same customer receives 9137, the vault creates a duplicate. The same defect appears when two independently seeded WWI instances are loaded.

**Minimal correction.** Assess each proposed hub key separately. Use a stable business identifier where one is evidenced. Where only a technical source identifier exists, label the result a provisional source-record key and qualify it by the actual generating authority and issuance regime (at least application instance plus entity/sequence where scopes are independent), preserving the source PK for lineage rather than claiming business identity.

**Confidence.** High.

## Finding 2

**Quoted row.** “state or province | Application.StateProvinces | country's `IsoAlpha3Code` + `StateProvinceCode` | ISO 3166-2 | one subdivision”.

**What is wrong.** `USA` + `WA` is not the ISO 3166-2 identifier. ISO 3166-2 uses the alpha-2 country prefix, here `US-WA`. Membership of `WA` in the set of US subdivision suffixes does not make the repository's invented alpha-3 composition an ISO-issued key. The row therefore both mislabels the namespace and departs from Rule 1's requirement that omission of a collision code be supported by a common governed namespace.

**Concrete failure case.** An external source loads Washington under canonical `US-WA`; WWI loads it under the proposed `USA|WA`. Hashing the asserted business keys creates two subdivision hub rows instead of integrating them.

**Minimal correction.** In the raw vault, use (`wwi`, `StateProvinceID`) unless a deterministic, governed crosswalk is accepted. If the ISO key is used, construct and store the canonical `US-WA` value from an evidenced alpha-2 country mapping and call that the ISO 3166-2 key; do not call `USA|WA` ISO 3166-2.

**Confidence.** High.

## Finding 3

**Quoted sentence.** “Reference tables are keyed the same way as hubs (Rule 1) and keep their history from the source's history tables, but get no hub; the tables that use them carry the code.”

**What is wrong.** The blanket “classifies, therefore no hub” test is not sufficient for Raw Data Vault modelling. At least stock group, buying group, and customer category participate in business relationships: stock-group membership has its own composite business key, and the three can define the scope of a special deal. A standard link is composed from hub keys; merely carrying a reference code either leaves those relationships without a hub endpoint or demotes relationship identity and history into attributes. The document acknowledges the relationships but treats the required endpoint as optional preference.

**Concrete failure case.** Stock item 10 leaves stock group 2 and later rejoins it while deals scoped to group 2 remain effective. Without a stock-group hub and an historized membership link, a consumer cannot reconstruct whether the item was in the governed deal scope at a prior instant without interpreting copied codes in several structures, and a rename or reuse of group 2 cannot be resolved once in one identity record.

**Minimal correction.** Promote a code set to a hub when its members are independently referenced by business relationships or own historized descriptive context; this requires at least stock group here and likely buying group and customer category if special-deal scope is loaded. Retain non-hub reference treatment only for codes used solely as descriptive domain values, and state that narrower criterion.

**Confidence.** High for stock group; medium for buying group and customer category pending the special-deal model.

## Finding 4

**Quoted sentence.** “An order line, an invoice line and a purchase-order line are identified only within their header.”

**What is wrong.** The cited wording says “a line on an Order”; it does not say the identifier is scoped by that order. For `OrderLineID`, the evidence actually observes a globally distinct, gapless value and says it is part of the primary key. The cited `(OrderID, StockItemID)` claim is only a proposed business-key candidate, not a declared constraint and not an observation stated by that claim. Calling the globally generated technical ID a dependent-child key does not establish the within-parent business key a dependent-child link needs.

**Concrete failure case.** Source row `OrderLineID=42` is corrected from `OrderID=10` to `OrderID=11`. A dependent-child identity keyed by (header, `OrderLineID`) records two distinct line identities, while the source treated one globally identified row as changing its relationship. Conversely, using (header, stock item) would collapse two legitimate same-item lines if the application later permits them.

**Minimal correction.** Do not infer parent scope. Either model `OrderLineID` explicitly as a source-issued record identifier and historize its header/stock-item relationships, or obtain an actual line number or other business key that is issued within the header. Apply the same evidence test independently to invoice and purchase-order lines.

**Confidence.** High on the evidential defect; medium on the final vault pattern because source update behaviour is not shown.

## Finding 5

**Quoted row and note.** “special deal | Sales.SpecialDeals | `SpecialDealID` | `wwi` | one pricing agreement” and “Proposed as a hub because a deal is an agreement with its own identity and its own validity period.”

**What is wrong.** The cited evidence establishes only a two-row technical identifier range, constraints, foreign keys, and effective dates. Effective dating does not prove that a deal has an independently used business key or “a life of its own,” and no other table refers to `SpecialDealID`. The six scope foreign keys instead suggest a relationship or rule whose identity is its participating scope, pricing option, and effective period. The hub decision therefore asserts precisely the business identity that the evidence does not establish.

**Concrete failure case.** An administrator deletes and re-enters the same pricing arrangement, producing `SpecialDealID=3` with the same scope and dates. The proposed hub reports two business agreements although the source activity may represent one corrected rule; the reverse failure occurs if an identity value is reused for a different rule after reload.

**Minimal correction.** Until a durable business identifier or independent reference is found, model `SpecialDealID` as a source technical record identifier attached to the deal-scope relationship/rule and keep its effective dates there. Promote it to a hub only when independent business identity is evidenced.

**Confidence.** High that the cited claims do not support the hub; medium on relationship-versus-rule placement.

## Citation overreach

Ten high-weight citations were checked: `Sales/Orders/06_keys.md#C1`; `Application/Countries/06_keys.md#C5` and `#C6`; `Application/StateProvinces/06_keys.md#C4` and `#C6`; `Sales/OrderLines/columns/OrderLineID/02_meaning.md#C1`; `Sales/OrderLines/06_keys.md#C3`; `Sales/SpecialDeals/03_temporal.md#C5`; `Sales/SpecialDeals/06_keys.md#C1`; and `Warehouse/StockItemStockGroups/06_keys.md#C4`.

- `Sales/Orders/06_keys.md#C1` says only that `OrderID` is the database primary key, has a source description, and has no other declared unique constraint. It does not establish a durable business key, an issuing authority, or an issuance regime; Rule 1 claims all three by adopting it under `wwi`.
- `Application/Countries/06_keys.md#C6` establishes that the observed pairs occur in a fetched current ISO list. It does not establish that WWI issues or governs the identifiers, nor that an observed list match alone warrants replacing the raw source key.
- `Application/StateProvinces/06_keys.md#C4` calls `StateProvinceCode` within country a medium-confidence “business key candidate”; section 3 promotes it to the business key without additional evidence.
- `Application/StateProvinces/06_keys.md#C6` establishes only that each two-letter suffix occurs in the ISO 3166-2:US list. It does not support the claimed alpha-3-plus-suffix ISO 3166-2 identifier.
- `Sales/OrderLines/columns/OrderLineID/02_meaning.md#C1` does not say that `OrderLineID` is unique only within an order; it merely quotes “a line on an Order.” Rule 3 claims parent scoping that the citation does not state.
- `Sales/OrderLines/06_keys.md#C3` labels `(OrderID, StockItemID)` an intended candidate. It does not say, as Rule 3 does, that the pair is observed unique.
- `Sales/SpecialDeals/03_temporal.md#C5` establishes bounded effective dates, not independent agreement identity.
- `Sales/SpecialDeals/06_keys.md#C1` establishes only that values 1 and 2 occur without a gap; it does not support “one pricing agreement” or hub-worthy identity.

FAILS [a source primary key can be reissued under a new WWI issuance regime, merging unrelated business objects under the single `wwi` key space].

## Dispositions

1. **Partly accepted.** Accepted: the keys are source-issued record identifiers, adopted because no business
   identifier is evidenced, and Rule 1 and the table now say so; and the key-space code names the issuer, the identity
   numbering of this database instance, so that a replacement application, a renumbering migration or a second instance
   gets a code of its own and a restarted sequence cannot merge two things. Not accepted: a code per entity or sequence
   inside the instance, because each concept has its own hub and numbers of different tables never meet; the three
   ledgers sharing one range do not overlap (`Sales/CustomerTransactions/06_keys.md#C3`).
2. **Accepted.** `USA|WA` is not an ISO 3166-2 identifier and the source cannot form `US-WA`. State or province is
   keyed on its record identifier, with the code kept as an attribute; weak case 1 narrows to the country.
3. **Accepted.** Rule 2 now makes a code list a hub when it takes part in a relationship of its own: stock group,
   buying group and customer category become hubs; six reference tables remain.
4. **Accepted.** The line identifiers are unique across all lines (`01_granularity.md#C1`, `#C2` of each line table),
   not within a header, and the observed (header, item) uniqueness is `01_granularity.md#C3`, not the intended
   candidate of `06_keys.md#C3`. Rule 3 now makes each line a hub on its own identifier, with header and item as links.
5. **Partly accepted.** Accepted: the record establishes no business identity for a deal, and the reason given ("an
   agreement with its own identity") is withdrawn. Not accepted: modelling the deal as a link, since its scope has
   exactly one of three customer-side and one of two item-side endpoints (check constraint, `Sales/SpecialDeals/06_keys.md#C3`);
   it stays a hub on its record identifier, like the lines, with the scope as links.
6. **Citation overreach, accepted where it applies.** `Sales/Orders/06_keys.md#C1` is now cited only for what it says,
   the identifier and its description, with the record-identifier label; `Application/StateProvinces/06_keys.md#C4`
   and `#C6` no longer carry a key; `Sales/OrderLines/columns/OrderLineID/02_meaning.md#C1` and `06_keys.md#C3` of the
   line tables are replaced by `01_granularity.md#C1` to `#C3`; `Sales/SpecialDeals/03_temporal.md#C5` no longer
   carries identity. Not accepted for `Application/Countries/06_keys.md#C6`: the namespace is governed by ISO, not by
   WWI, which is what 144 §3 asks for.
