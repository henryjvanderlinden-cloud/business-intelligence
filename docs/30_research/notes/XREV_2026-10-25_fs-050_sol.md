## Pass 1 — sol's answer, verbatim

## (a) Cases where the identity fails to close

1. **Quoted sentence:** “Loan sub-ledger postings, after allocation, are what reconcile to the position.”
   **What is wrong:** A position and a posting population reconcile only when they use the same balance component and ledger account. Concrete case: capitalised interest is posted as a debit to principal and a credit to accrued interest. Summing both sides of the sub-ledger journal gives zero while principal outstanding rises by the capitalised amount. Every stated decision can be followed and the principal roll-forward still fails.
   **Correction:** Define a separate roll-forward for each balance component and currency, select only the postings that debit or credit that component, and normalise their signs. Reconcile the component roll-forwards to the corresponding components of the closing position before deriving any total.
   **Confidence:** High.

2. **Quoted sentence:** “Loan sub-ledger postings, after allocation, are what reconcile to the position.”
   **What is wrong:** Some servicing platforms change a carried balance through a balance conversion, migration opening, contract-state correction, or end-of-day calculation without emitting an ordinary loan-posting record into the supplied transaction feed. Concrete case: during a servicing migration, a loan part closes October at EUR 180,000 and opens on the new platform in November at EUR 180,012 after a corrected conversion balance; the November posting extract contains no EUR 12 transaction. The two snapshots cannot be bridged from that extract.
   **Correction:** Prove that every state mutation emits an event into the captured source population. Where it does not, ingest balance-adjustment, conversion and calculation journals as separate source events, with lineage; otherwise classify the difference as an unreconciled control exception, not an economic movement.
   **Confidence:** High.

3. **Quoted sentence:** “Resolve the mapping explicitly in a link.”
   **What is wrong:** A relationship does not allocate an amount. Concrete case: one EUR 900 contract-level receipt is allocated by the servicer as EUR 500 scheduled principal on part A, EUR 300 arrears cure on part B and EUR 100 interest. A link from the contract posting to all three parts does not determine those amounts, so neither part-level identity closes.
   **Correction:** Capture the source allocation lines where they exist. Otherwise create a versioned allocation rule and an allocation result at posting-line × loan-part × balance-component grain, preserving the unallocated amount as an exception.
   **Confidence:** High.

4. **Quoted sentence:** “Per loan it closes the identity: opening balance + Σ movements = closing balance.”
   **What is wrong:** The identity assumes stable identity across the period. Concrete case: a restructuring extinguishes loan parts A and B and creates part C under a new identifier with the same EUR 250,000 debt. If the source records only a contract conversion or transfer, A and B appear to fall to zero and C appears with a balance, without part-level economic postings that bridge either side.
   **Correction:** Model extinguishment, creation, split, merge and identifier succession explicitly. Reconcile stable parts individually and reconcile restructurings through a transfer bridge whose outgoing and incoming amounts balance at the common contract or obligation level.
   **Confidence:** High.

5. **Quoted sentence:** “Report back-dated corrections separately, as restatement deltas against the earlier as-of view.”
   **What is wrong:** A restatement delta does not close a roll-forward unless the opening and closing snapshots are pinned to compatible knowledge versions. Concrete case: the stored October opening used in November is silently replaced by a restated October balance of EUR 99,900, while the published October close was EUR 100,000. November postings of EUR -1,000 and a November close of EUR 98,900 close against the restated opening but fail by EUR 100 against the published opening.
   **Correction:** Give every reconciliation an explicit valid period and assertion/cut-off timestamp. Either restate both the prior close and the roll-forward, or retain the published opening and include a separately identified prior-period adjustment in the current bridge; never mix versions.
   **Confidence:** High.

6. **Quoted sentence:** “Build a fact at the grain instrument × period × movement type.”
   **What is wrong:** Currency and measurement precision are absent from the grain. Concrete case: three postings of EUR 0.004 are retained at posting precision, while each monthly position is rounded to cents. Their sum is EUR 0.012 but the reported closing change is EUR 0.01, leaving EUR 0.002 even though all events are present and classified.
   **Correction:** Add currency, balance component, measurement basis and, where necessary, ledger/book to the grain. Preserve source precision and apply one documented rounding policy at the same aggregation level as the reported position; record rounding differences separately from unexplained differences.
   **Confidence:** High.

## (b) Errors in taxonomy, membership, time basis, or loan-part grain

1. **Quoted sentence:** “Map raw transaction codes to securitisation categories: scheduled principal; partial and full prepayment; further advance; capitalisation; arrears change; write-off and recovery; repurchase; substitution; pool addition and removal.”
   **What is wrong:** The list mixes changes to different stocks. Scheduled principal and prepayment change principal; arrears change usually reclassifies an amount between current and delinquent buckets without changing total principal; pool addition and removal change reporting perimeter without changing the loan; recovery may be cash or income after the exposure has already been written off. Adding all of them to one loan balance double-counts or invents changes.
   **Correction:** Define the measured stock first and maintain separate, balanced bridges for principal, accrued interest, fees, arrears buckets, impairment/write-off balances, linked savings balances and pool perimeter. Do not sum reclassifications or perimeter events into the legal-principal bridge.
   **Confidence:** High.

2. **Quoted sentence:** “scheduled principal” and “partial and full prepayment”
   **What is wrong:** A raw posting code normally cannot distinguish scheduled principal from prepayment. Concrete case: EUR 1,000 received after two missed EUR 500 instalments can be an arrears cure, the current scheduled instalment, or a prepayment depending on the contractual schedule and the servicer’s allocation waterfall. “Full” is also a resulting state, not necessarily a posting type.
   **Correction:** Classify principal cash by comparing due amounts, contractual schedule, arrears state, allocation sequence and resulting balance as of the payment’s effective date. Keep source posting type separate from the derived investor-reporting classification.
   **Confidence:** High.

3. **Quoted sentence:** “write-off and recovery”
   **What is wrong:** Write-off and recovery are not one movement type and need not affect the same measure. A write-off can reduce accounting gross carrying amount or an allowance while leaving contractual principal due; a later recovery on a written-off loan can be cash and income without reinstating principal.
   **Correction:** Separate contractual balance, gross carrying amount, loss allowance, write-off, post-write-off recovery cash and recovery income. Give write-off and recovery distinct categories and state which roll-forward each affects.
   **Confidence:** High.

4. **Quoted sentence:** “Payments explain cash. Balances also move for non-cash reasons: interest capitalisation, fees, write-offs, restructurings, FX and accounting adjustments.”
   **What is wrong:** The controlled taxonomy that follows omits fees, FX, reversals, technical transfers and generic accounting corrections even though the text says they move balances. An unmapped fee capitalisation, reversal of an October principal posting, or legacy non-euro mortgage translation therefore falls into the residual.
   **Correction:** Make the taxonomy exhaustive for each chosen balance measure, including reversal/cancellation and technical transfer as distinct semantics. Reject unmapped source codes into a governed exception queue; do not use the residual as their normal category.
   **Confidence:** High.

5. **Quoted sentence:** “Entering and leaving the pool (sale, repurchase, substitution, redemption) are movement types in their own right.”
   **What is wrong:** These are not movements of a loan balance. A EUR 200,000 loan can leave the pool at EUR 200,000 while its own opening and closing principal are both EUR 200,000. Putting a EUR -200,000 removal into its loan roll-forward makes that identity false. Conversely, using a repurchase price can include accrued interest or premium and will not equal the removed principal.
   **Correction:** Keep the per-loan economic balance roll-forward independent of eligibility and ownership. Build a separate pool-perimeter bridge that adds the balance on entry and removes the balance on exit, measured on the same reporting basis and effective instant as the pool snapshots; keep sale or repurchase consideration separate.
   **Confidence:** High.

6. **Quoted sentence:** “Otherwise the pool-level reconciliation breaks whenever the composition changes.”
   **What is wrong:** Treating membership events as ordinary movements does not by itself make the pool equation well-defined. A loan entering exactly at month-end may be included in the closing pool snapshot but its same-day economic postings may or may not belong to the pool, depending on the contractual cut-off and whether boundaries are inclusive. The proposed model supplies no rule.
   **Correction:** Define opening and closing population predicates, effective timestamps, time zone, inclusivity, and the attribution of movements on entry and exit dates. Express the pool bridge as opening-population balance + economic movements while in-scope + entry balances − exit balances = closing-population balance.
   **Confidence:** High.

7. **Quoted sentence:** “Reconcile positions and transactions on the same axis, preferably transaction time: what was known at month-end close.”
   **What is wrong:** “What was known” is an assertion or knowledge cut-off, not the economic time axis of the balance. Month-end contractual positions are normally effective as of a business/value date, while transaction time says when a record was booked or observed. Concrete case: a EUR 10,000 repayment received and value-dated 31 October is booked on 2 November. The 31 October economic position may include it after restatement; grouping movements by November transaction time cannot explain that October position and can also distort November.
   **Correction:** Reconcile by business-effective/value date, using the same end-of-day convention as the position. Independently select the source versions known by a stated assertion cut-off. Produce both an original-as-published and a latest-restated reconciliation when required.
   **Confidence:** High.

8. **Quoted sentence:** “Report back-dated corrections separately, as restatement deltas against the earlier as-of view.”
   **What is wrong:** “Separately” leaves the delta outside the equation it must repair and conflates corrections with late-arriving valid events. A late-booked 31 October repayment is an October effective movement in a restated October roll-forward; a correction to its amount is a new assertion about that event. They are not the same category.
   **Correction:** Store effective time and assertion time for every event and snapshot. Recompute the effective-period bridge as of each reporting cut-off, and derive the restatement delta by comparing two complete as-of results; do not classify all late knowledge as a current-period movement.
   **Confidence:** High.

9. **Quoted sentence:** “Securitisation reporting sits at loan-part level; for Dutch mortgages, that means individual loan parts (leningdelen) with different redemption types.”
   **What is wrong:** Loan part is necessary for many Dutch product attributes but is not universally the source or reporting grain. Collections, arrears, collateral, foreclosure proceeds and some investor pools can be held or allocated at mortgage-contract, borrower-account or collateral-set level. Merely declaring loan-part grain creates invented allocations and can lose joint-and-several or cross-part effects.
   **Correction:** Preserve each source event at its native grain. Use explicit allocation results only where the reporting contract requires part-level amounts, retain unallocated contract-level amounts, and reconcile both part totals and their parent mortgage contract.
   **Confidence:** High.

10. **Quoted sentence:** “further advance”
    **What is wrong:** In Dutch servicing, a further advance can create a new leningdeel with its own redemption form, rate and start date rather than increase an existing part. Treating it as a movement of the existing instrument makes the old part rise without a posting or makes the new part appear without an opening balance.
    **Correction:** Distinguish a draw on an existing part from origination of a new part. Represent the latter as a new instrument entering the contract, with its own opening/disbursement event and any associated pool-entry decision.
    **Confidence:** High.

11. **Quoted sentence:** “Savings and bank-savings mortgages need extra care, because the linked savings balance moves separately from the loan balance.”
    **What is wrong:** “Extra care” does not resolve the essential accounting distinction. During the term, contributions and credited return increase a pledged savings account or insurance-policy value but ordinarily do not amortise the legal mortgage principal. Insurance premiums may also include risk and cost components that never enter the savings value. Netting savings growth against principal each month would fabricate principal repayments; ignoring the linked asset would fail a net-exposure report.
    **Correction:** Model the mortgage loan part and the bank-savings account or insurance-policy value as separate instruments, with separately sourced movements, ownership/provider, pledge relationship and valuation basis. Do not net them unless the specific investor definition requires it. On maturity or permitted application, record the actual savings proceeds and the separate principal-redemption allocation.
    **Confidence:** High.

## (c) Errors in the Data Vault placement table

1. **Quoted sentence:** “Postings | Non-historised link”
   **What is wrong:** This placement is only safe for an immutable posting event whose complete identity includes every discriminator. Posting amount, currency, debit/credit account, value date, booking date, status, reversal reference and source version are not merely a relationship, and several can be corrected. A bare non-historised link either loses that payload and its bitemporal history or embeds mutable descriptive data in the link.
   **Correction:** Preserve an immutable posting/event business key in a transaction or non-historised link only for the immutable event grain, attach payload and source-version history in the appropriate satellite(s), and record corrections and reversals as new assertions/events linked to the original. Carry both effective and load/assertion timestamps.
   **Confidence:** Medium.

2. **Quoted sentence:** “Positions | Snapshot satellites on the Instrument hub”
   **What is wrong:** Instrument alone is not the parent grain of a position observation. One instrument can have simultaneous principal, interest, fee, allowance, gross/net, ledger, currency and reporting-basis positions for the same date. A conventional satellite on the hub cannot distinguish those concurrent observations merely by treating them as successive descriptions of the instrument.
   **Correction:** Model a position observation at instrument × effective timestamp × balance component × currency × ledger/reporting basis, using a multi-active/periodic-snapshot pattern or an observation link and satellites appropriate to the repository’s chosen Data Vault standard. Retain assertion/load versions rather than overwriting a month-end observation.
   **Confidence:** High.

3. **Quoted sentence:** “Month-end views | PIT tables”
   **What is wrong:** A PIT table is a query-performance aid that resolves which satellite rows were current at an as-of load time; it is not the accounting month-end fact, the business-effective cut-off rule, or an independently retained published view. Calling a month-end view a PIT conflates valid time with load time.
   **Correction:** Store the underlying bitemporal observations and define a reproducible month-end selection with effective-date, close-calendar and assertion-cut-off rules. Use PIT tables only as rebuildable accelerators for the relevant satellite-version joins; retain published snapshot identity separately where reports must be reproduced.
   **Confidence:** High.

4. **Quoted sentence:** “Movement fact and residual | Derived in the business vault”
   **What is wrong:** The economic movements may be derived business-vault objects, but the residual is a control result, not a movement. Including `closing − opening − classified movements` as a movement makes closure tautological and falsely permits it into movement waterfalls and investor reports.
   **Correction:** Keep sourced and rule-derived economic movements separate from a reconciliation-result object. Store the residual with the two compared snapshot versions, rule-set version, cut-off, run identifier and status; exclude it from economic movement totals and reporting unless explicitly labelled as an unresolved difference.
   **Confidence:** High.

5. **Quoted sentence:** “Reconciliation mart | Reads only from the movement fact, so the identity stays auditable end to end”
   **What is wrong:** A mart that reads only the movement fact cannot independently compare opening and closing source positions, prove posting completeness, or expose mapping and allocation lineage. If the same fact contains the computed residual, it will always close by construction and is not an end-to-end audit.
   **Correction:** Make the reconciliation consume independently versioned opening and closing observations, the complete in-scope posting population, membership/perimeter events, and versioned classification and allocation results. Publish source counts and amounts, unmapped and unallocated populations, the residual, and lineage to each side of the equation.
   **Confidence:** High.

6. **Quoted sentence:** “omission”
   **What is wrong:** The table contains no placement for pool membership/effectivity, posting-to-part allocation, taxonomy rule versions, source-code reference data, restatement assertions, or reconciliation runs, even though those objects carry the logic needed to reproduce the result. “Derived in the business vault” is not enough to make their histories or keys auditable.
   **Correction:** Place each explicitly: raw membership relationships and their asserted/effective history; immutable allocation inputs plus versioned business-vault allocation results; historised source-code reference data and rule-set versions; and a reconciliation-run/control-result object keyed by period, cut-off, rule set and input versions.
   **Confidence:** High.

verdict - FAILS + pool entry and exit are treated as loan-balance movements, so an unchanged EUR 200,000 loan removed from the pool acquires a fictitious EUR -200,000 movement and cannot satisfy its own roll-forward

## Pass 2 — sol's answer, verbatim

1. **Pool-perimeter bridge: the entry/exit valuation instant is still wrong or incomplete.** Section 3 says entry and exit are measured “at the same effective instant as the pool snapshots”. They must instead be measured at the membership boundary: entry balance immediately before the first movement attributed to the pool, and exit balance immediately after the last such movement, under one explicit ordering rule. Concrete case: a loan enters on 15 July at EUR 100 and amortises EUR 10 on 20 July, so the 31 July pool position is EUR 90. If “entry balance” is measured at the closing-snapshot instant, it is EUR 90; the stated equation gives `0 - 10 + 90 = 80`, not EUR 90. The equation closes only with the EUR 100 boundary balance. The same defect can double-count a movement sharing an entry timestamp unless the balance is designated pre- or post-movement; time zone, inclusivity and movement attribution alone do not designate that valuation side.

2. **The native-grain finding is applied only partially.** Section 4.5.1 correctly says to keep each source event at its native grain, but section 2.2 still defines the roll-forward grain as loan part, and the Positions row in section 5 hard-codes loan part rather than instrument or native position grain. Concrete case: a servicer carries one EUR 900 recovery-cash position and its movements at mortgage-contract level for two loan parts and supplies no part allocation. Preserving that source fact permits a contract-level bridge; forcing the movement fact and position observation to loan-part grain either invents an allocation or leaves both part bridges unable to close. “Reconcile both the part totals and the parent contract” cannot be implemented from a position model that admits only loan-part observations. The roll-forward grain must allow the measured instrument/native grain, with loan-part allocation as a separate result only where required.

3. **The time-basis repair omits one of the two treatments needed for a published series.** Section 4.1.4 says never to mix a restated opening with a published close, and section 4.1.5 says to compare complete original-as-published and latest-restated results, but it does not put a prior-period adjustment into the original published-series equation. Concrete case: the published 31 October close is EUR 100,000; a correction asserted on 5 November and effective 31 October reduces it to EUR 99,900; November has EUR 1,000 of effective-period repayments; and the published 30 November close is EUR 98,900. The latest-restated bridge closes (`99,900 - 1,000 = 98,900`). The unrevised published-series bridge does not (`100,000 - 1,000 = 99,000`). Merely comparing the two complete results identifies EUR 100 but does not close the latter equation. If the prior published close is retained, the bridge needs an explicitly identified EUR -100 prior-period adjustment; alternatively the prior close and the whole bridge must be restated. Such an adjustment is not a November economic movement, so the warning against putting late knowledge into “current-period movement” does not supply it.

verdict - FAILS + a loan entering at EUR 100, amortising EUR 10 after entry and closing at EUR 90 produces EUR 80 under the stated pool bridge when entry balance is measured at the closing-snapshot instant
