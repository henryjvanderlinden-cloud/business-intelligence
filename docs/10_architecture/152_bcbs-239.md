# 152 — BCBS 239: binding for a bank in production, a reference for other financial institutions

*Drafted 2026-10-15, session 08, on the author's instruction, and open as `ARC-OP-35` in the
[register](../../registers/OPEN_POINTS.md). The same day the author ruled on its scope and on items 15 and 16, recorded
in [ADR-0016](../20_decisions/0016-regulatory-requirements-from-production.md). Unreviewed. Subjunctive throughout:
nothing described here is built, and the other consequences in §4 are proposals until the author rules on them.*

The author's instruction, verbatim:

> I’d like to make a note somewhere that the principles of BCBS 239 must be followed when working for a financial
> institution.

## 1. What the rule does to a generic framework

The framework is generic ([ADR-0012](../20_decisions/0012-generic-framework-lean-start.md)) and written for no
particular organisation. This rule does not change that. It adds a condition: when an implementation for a bank is
in production, the BCBS 239 principles are a floor it must meet, not a consideration to be weighed against others.
In the terms of the [adjudication economy](155_adjudication-economy.md) it is a hard rule, and 155 already sends "anything regulatory" to a person by mandate, outside the optimisation. §4 sets out what the
rule would change elsewhere in the design.

**Scope, as ruled.** BCBS 239 is addressed to banks, and the author confirmed on 2026-10-15 that it applies to them
only, "but it's inspiration for e.g. other credit institutions". So: for a bank, the principles are followed; for
another financial institution they are a reference to design against, and the regime that actually applies to it is
followed. **When, as ruled.** Regulatory requirements are kept in mind from the start, are not required to be met in
a proof of concept or a pilot, and apply in full once the system is in production. The author's words, verbatim:

> connection is back. BCBS 239 only applies to banks, indeed, but it's inspiration for e.g. other credit institutions.
> I think 15 is not that far of a stretch. Let's say that regulatory requirements need to be kept in mind when starting
> lean, although compliance is not necessary when still in a proof of concept or pilot phase. Once a system is in
> production though, regulatory requirements apply. So best to keep those considerations in mind from the start. Item
> 16: as is customary, as we say in Dutch "nood breekt wet". If you make reference of this, keep it brief. It is
> obvious and distracting, although for an LLM it might be necessary to make it explicit ;-).

Both rulings are recorded in [ADR-0016](../20_decisions/0016-regulatory-requirements-from-production.md).

Throughout, **in-scope data** means the risk data, and the data behind the risk reports and key risk models, that
the institution has placed within the scope of its BCBS 239 framework. Deciding that scope is the institution's act,
not the system's.

## 2. What BCBS 239 is

The facts in this section come from the sources listed at the end, read on 2026-10-15.

The Basel Committee on Banking Supervision published *Principles for effective risk data aggregation and risk
reporting* on 9 January 2013; it is known by its publication number, BCBS 239. It sets out fourteen principles in
four groups. It was aimed first at global systemically important banks: those designated in 2011 or 2012 were to
comply by January 2016, and banks designated later within three years of designation. National supervisors are
encouraged to apply the principles to domestic systemically important banks three years after designation, and may
extend them proportionately to other banks. The principles apply at group and at solo level, and also to the data
used in a bank's key internal risk models.

*Risk data aggregation* is defined there as defining, gathering and processing risk data according to the bank's
risk reporting requirements, so that the bank can measure its performance against its risk tolerance or appetite;
it includes sorting, merging and breaking down sets of data.

The principles are not a solved problem in the industry. The Basel Committee's progress report of 28 November 2023
assessed 31 global systemically important banks and found that additional work was required at all of them to
attain or sustain full compliance. Among its recommendations: boards should take full responsibility for data
governance, and banks should apply the principles more comprehensively than to risk reporting alone.

For banks supervised directly by the European Central Bank, the ECB published a *Guide on effective risk data
aggregation and risk reporting* on 3 May 2024, to be read alongside BCBS 239. It names seven areas: the management
body's responsibility, the scope of application of the data governance framework, key roles and responsibilities,
a group-wide integrated data architecture, the effectiveness of data quality controls, the timeliness of internal
risk reporting, and implementation programmes. A data-lineage vendor's reading of the ECB's feedback statement on
the draft guide reports that lineage is expected at the level of the data attribute, from data capture to
reporting, and that it should include every manual step and the data quality controls at each step. That reading is
second-hand and from an interested party; it is to be checked against the guide itself before anything is built on
it.

## 3. The fourteen principles

Paraphrased from the BCBS text; paragraph numbers are the document's own.

| No. | Principle | What it asks, in short |
|---|---|---|
| | **I. Overarching governance and infrastructure** | |
| 1 | Governance | Strong governance over risk data aggregation and risk reporting, owned by the board and senior management, and subject to high standards of validation that is independent of those who build it (¶29). |
| 2 | Data architecture and IT infrastructure | An architecture and infrastructure that support aggregation and reporting in stress and crisis as well as in normal times; integrated data taxonomies across the group (¶33); data ownership defined for both business and IT (¶34). |
| | **II. Risk data aggregation capabilities** | |
| 3 | Accuracy and integrity | Accurate and reliable risk data, aggregated on a largely automated basis to minimise errors (¶35); reconciled with its sources, including accounting data where appropriate (¶36c); a single authoritative source per type of risk as the aim (¶36d); a dictionary of concepts used consistently across the organisation (¶37); every aggregation process documented, manual workarounds included, with their criticality explained (¶39); accuracy measured and monitored, with escalation (¶40). |
| 4 | Completeness | All material risk data across the group, available by business line, legal entity, asset type, industry, region and other groupings relevant to the risk; omissions identified and explained. |
| 5 | Timeliness | Aggregate, up-to-date risk data in good time, the timing set by the nature and volatility of the risk and how critical it is, and quickly in stress. |
| 6 | Adaptability | Answers to a broad range of on-demand and ad hoc requests, including in stress or crisis, for internal needs and for supervisors. |
| | **III. Risk reporting practices** | |
| 7 | Accuracy | Reports that convey the aggregated data accurately and precisely, reconciled and validated; automated and manual edit and reasonableness checks, with an inventory of the validation rules (¶53b); data errors identified, reported and explained through exception reports (¶53c). |
| 8 | Comprehensiveness | Reports that cover all material risk areas, in depth and scope suited to the bank's size, complexity and risk profile and to what the recipients need. |
| 9 | Clarity and usefulness | Reports that are clear and concise, yet comprehensive enough to decide on, and tailored to their recipients. |
| 10 | Frequency | Reporting frequency set by the board and senior management, by the recipients' needs and how fast the risk can change, and raised in stress. |
| 11 | Distribution | Reports that reach the relevant parties promptly, with confidentiality maintained. |
| | **IV. Supervisory review, tools and cooperation** | |
| 12 | Review | Supervisors review and evaluate a bank's compliance periodically. |
| 13 | Remedial actions and supervisory measures | Supervisors have, and use, the tools to require timely remediation. |
| 14 | Home/host cooperation | Supervisors cooperate across jurisdictions. |

The principles are meant to be met all at once. Trade-offs between them are accepted only in exceptional
circumstances, such as an urgent ad hoc request about a new or unknown risk; they must not materially affect risk
management decisions, and the bank must have a policy for them and be able to explain their impact (¶22).

Principles 12 to 14 address supervisors, not banks. They bind the institution only through what it must be able
to show: evidence for a review, and the capacity to remediate quickly.

## 4. What it would mean for this design

Read against the design as it stands, BCBS 239 confirms much of it, tightens several parts, and qualifies two
rulings. The items are numbered so that they can be pointed at. Each names the part of the design concerned, the
principles that bear on it, and the consequence. An item that changes an accepted ADR would need an ADR of its own
before it binds.

### Where the design already points the right way

1. **Models out of the data path** ([ADR-0004](../20_decisions/0004-models-out-of-the-data-path.md),
   [100](100_system-overview.md)). Principles 3 and 7 ask for accurate data, aggregated largely automatically,
   reconciled and explainable. A design in which every number is produced by deterministic code from versioned
   declarations, and a past period can be reproduced exactly, is the strongest position available on both. For a
   financial institution the one law would harden further: ADR-0004's exception — model-based row processing as a
   separately evaluated enrichment step, treated as a source — would not feed in-scope data unless the institution's
   own validation had approved it, as it would any model whose output enters a risk figure.

2. **Everything regenerable from declarations under version control** ([100](100_system-overview.md),
   [150](150_trust-and-blast-radius.md), [180](180_technology-posture.md), and the author's wish for a plain-text
   codebase of many small files, `ARC-OP-20`). Principles 2 and 3 ask for a documented architecture and documented
   aggregation processes. A system whose warehouse can be rebuilt from its declarations holds most of that
   documentation by construction. The consequence is that column-level lineage, which
   [160](160_evaluation-and-regression.md) already assumes for release regression, stops being a convenience and
   becomes a deliverable; and it would have to reach beyond the warehouse — back to data capture in the source, and
   forward into whatever report or end-user tool consumes a mart — because lineage that stops at the warehouse
   boundary would not meet the expectation reported in §2. OpenLineage, a candidate in 180, is the obvious format.

3. **All the data, all the history, integrated on business keys**
   ([ADR-0013](../20_decisions/0013-data-vault-or-equivalent.md), [127](127_temporal-behaviour.md),
   [190](190_machine-authored-design-canon.md) §1). Principle 4 asks for completeness across the group with
   breakdowns by business line, legal entity, asset type, industry and region; Principle 6 for answers to ad hoc
   questions, including under stress; and a supervisor will ask what was reported on a past date. An insert-only
   layer integrated on business keys can answer all three, and 190's three questions — what we now believe was true
   then, what we reported then, and what we would have reported then knowing what we know now — are close to the
   questions a review asks. Two consequences. Legal entity, business line and the other groupings would be
   first-class dimensions of the data model ([142](142_the-data-model.md) §4) from the start, not attributes added
   later. And the capture guarantee of 127 carries more weight: a history labelled *sampled* would not be acceptable
   for in-scope data unless the institution has decided, in writing, that it is.

4. **The publication gate** ([ADR-0011](../20_decisions/0011-assertions-identity-plane-publication-gate.md)).
   Principle 7 asks that reports be reconciled and validated before they are relied on. A gate that releases a mart
   only when the tests on its data version have passed is that control in mechanical form. Item 16 below names the
   one way it could work against the principles.

5. **Whoever makes a change does not accept it** ([160](160_evaluation-and-regression.md), *Release regression*;
   [520](../50_process/520_record-keeping.md)). Principle 1 asks for validation independent of those who build. The
   release-regression rule that acceptance is made by someone other than the change's author has that shape. The
   consequence is a limit: cross-model review is not independent validation in BCBS 239's sense. A second model
   reviewing a first is a quality measure inside the build; the validation the principles ask for is done by people
   independent of the build, and the system's part is to give them the evidence.

6. **The machinery works without the agents** (the author's ruling of 2026-10-07,
   [420](../40_roadmap/420_next-sandbox-setup.md) §5). Principle 2 asks that the infrastructure support aggregation
   in stress and crisis. A risk report that cannot be produced because a model provider is down, rate-limited or
   unavailable would fail it. For a financial institution the ruling would become a requirement: every in-scope load,
   test and report runs without the agentic layer, which builds and maintains the machinery and never runs it.

7. **Source contracts, read-only access, a bounded blast radius** ([150](150_trust-and-blast-radius.md)). The design
   never writes to a source and reads only under contract, which fits Principle 1 and the confidentiality of
   Principle 11. An adjacent consequence, outside BCBS 239 but certain to be raised beside it: an agentic layer that
   sends metadata or samples to an outside model provider is, at a bank, a matter for the institution's outsourcing
   and ICT third-party rules — in the European Union, among others, the Digital Operational Resilience Act (named from
   general knowledge, not checked in this session). The source contract would have to record what may leave the
   institution, and to whom.

### Where BCBS 239 would tighten the design

8. **The autonomy tiers** ([105](105_autonomy-tiers.md), [ADR-0005](../20_decisions/0005-three-autonomy-tiers.md)).
   Tier 1 is unaffected: measuring is measuring. Tier 2 lets a hypothesis become an assertion on evidence alone once
   the evidence crosses a threshold. For in-scope data that would not be allowed for the claims Principle 3 turns on:
   which source is authoritative for a type of risk (¶36d), what a concept means (¶37), and which identifier denotes
   an entity. Those would be ruled by the owner the institution's data governance names (¶34). At Tier 3 the decision
   register would not be built from nothing: it would start from the institution's own risk reporting requirements,
   and the system could not decide to leave out a material risk area, since Principle 8 requires all of them and an
   omission must be explainable as immaterial.

9. **The lanes of the adjudication economy** ([155](155_adjudication-economy.md)). The auto-accept and provisional
   lanes rest on reversibility and limited reach. A claim that reaches a risk report has neither in the sense that
   matters: once a board has acted on a figure, retracting the claim does not retract the decision. So, for in-scope
   data, the provisional lane would be closed to any claim that reaches a risk report; when a human-lane deadline
   passes, the default could not be *stays provisional within its scope* but would be *not used in in-scope
   reporting*, with the gap stated; and whether a claim touches in-scope data would be checked independently, as 155
   already asks of every hard rule. The economic consequence is that a larger share of claims would reach people than
   the design assumes, which bears directly on `CON-OP-2`: at a bank, mandatory review could take most of the weekly
   budget of human minutes.

10. **Reconciliation** ([162](162_test-framework.md)). 162 treats reconciliation against a verified report — the
    books, external reporting — as a periodic data test, run when such a report exists. Principle 3 asks that risk
    data be reconciled with its sources, accounting data included where appropriate, and differences explained
    (¶36c). For in-scope data it would be mandatory, run at least as often as the report it supports, and an
    unexplained difference would block publication rather than warn.

11. **Plausibility stays soft, but every warning gets a disposition** ([160](160_evaluation-and-regression.md),
    *Plausibility*; 162). Soft, back-of-the-envelope checks are what Principle 7 calls reasonableness checks (¶53b),
    so their design need not change. Two things would: the checks would be kept in an inventory of validation rules
    with an owner for each, which ¶53b asks for; and a warning on in-scope data could not wait until someone happens
    to open it, as 160 now allows. Each would be closed with a recorded explanation before the report goes out, or
    carried in the report as a known limitation, which is the exception reporting of ¶53c.

12. **Manual work is inventoried, not only minimised** (the author's aim, `EVI-OP-7`;
    [520](../50_process/520_record-keeping.md)). The project's aim is to minimise manual work, and the step log
    records it for the project's own purposes. Principle 3 asks that every aggregation process, manual workarounds
    included, be documented with its criticality explained (¶39). At an institution that would become a production
    inventory: every manual adjustment, override, top-side entry or repair on the path to a risk report, recorded with
    who, when, why and its effect, and reported as a weakness when material. The aim and the inventory point the same
    way; the inventory is what makes a reduction demonstrable.

13. **The institution's dictionary is authoritative** ([120](120_context-layer.md),
    [ADR-0011](../20_decisions/0011-assertions-identity-plane-publication-gate.md), [180](180_technology-posture.md)).
    Principle 3 asks for a dictionary of concepts used consistently across the organisation (¶37). The context system
    holds exactly that kind of knowledge, but a bank working under BCBS 239 will already have an official glossary and
    dictionary, owned by named people. 180 already treats existing catalogues as sources to read rather than
    substrates to adopt; for a financial institution the rule would be stronger: the institution's dictionary is
    authoritative, an assertion that contradicts it goes to the human lane as a contradiction, and the system's
    catalogue never presents itself as a second dictionary. Its publication pipeline would feed the official one, or
    feed nothing.

14. **Pruning respects what was reported** ([145](145_entropy-and-pruning.md)). Evidence that nobody uses a mart is
    enough to retire it. It is not enough to retire anything that has fed a risk report, because the institution must
    still be able to say what it reported and how the figure was produced. There, pruning would remove the running
    object but keep the declarations and context versions that rebuild it, and would need the owner's sign-off.

### Where BCBS 239 qualifies a ruling

15. **Every implementation starts lean** ([ADR-0012](../20_decisions/0012-generic-framework-lean-start.md)).
    *Ruled 2026-10-15, [ADR-0016](../20_decisions/0016-regulatory-requirements-from-production.md).* A proof of concept
    or a pilot may start lean without meeting BCBS 239; a system in production must meet it. The requirements are kept
    in mind from the start, so that the controls a lean start postpones — the quality gates of `ARC-OP-33`, postponed in
    the Wide World Importers sandbox on 2026-10-11, the publication gate, reconciliation, lineage, the inventory of
    manual steps — are designed in, and can be switched on before the first in-scope report goes to production rather
    than retrofitted.

16. **Necessity overrides the gate** ([ADR-0011](../20_decisions/0011-assertions-identity-plane-publication-gate.md);
    ¶22). *Ruled 2026-10-15.* In an emergency a risk report goes out despite a failed test — *nood breekt wet* — with
    the failure stated in it and the release decided and recorded by a named person, never by an agent.

### What an institution could gain

17. **The evidence is a by-product.** Not a requirement but an observation, and a hypothesis rather than a finding:
    the evidence a BCBS 239 self-assessment or supervisory review asks for — a documented architecture, attribute-level
    lineage, the dictionary, an inventory of controls and manual steps, reconciliation results, and the history of
    every change with who approved it — is what this design would hold anyway. The 2023 progress report suggests the
    need is real. Whether that is a value area in the sense of
    [ADR-0009](../20_decisions/0009-separate-capabilities-open-value-areas.md) is for the author.

## 5. What a sandbox cannot show

The Wide World Importers sandbox (`EVI-OP-7`) simulates a wholesaler. It has no risk data, no board, no supervisor and
no reconciliation of the kind BCBS 239 means. As [ADR-0012](../20_decisions/0012-generic-framework-lean-start.md)
requires, that is recorded so that it is not mistaken for something tested: nothing in this note has been exercised.

## 6. Not decided

Which of items 8 to 14 are adopted, those that change ADR-0005 or 155 by ADR; checking the ECB guide's lineage expectation
against the guide itself; and review on a second model, per
